# Epic 4 decisions

D-124 to D-179 belong to Epic 5.

What the PRD, the addendum, the mock and the code leave open or state twice for v0.4, settled while writing the Epic 4 stories on 2026-10-28 under the owner's delegation of design decisions, then reworked the same day after a review of the stories: D-180 to D-189 are new, and a decision edited in place names the finding (`review Fn`) that changed it. ## Plan challenge

The Epic 4 plan (this file's D-460 to D-485, the PRD, the architecture, stories 4.1 to 4.12 as then numbered, and the v0.5 and v0.7 addenda) was challenged on 2026-10-28 (not published: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/qa/epic-4-plan-challenge.md`). The owner decided its main points; the others were settled under his delegation, each applied when it is a clear improvement and otherwise recorded with the reason. "Challenge" below names the report's section.

- **D-494.** A stale or unknown board never clears an alert (owner decision; challenge, Serious "Stale data clears alerts"; amends AD-167 rules 1 and 2, D-465, D-473, D-475, D-482). A card placed `unknown` by rule 1 (`board.stale`) or rule 2 (the `unknown` badge) keeps the `alert` and `waiting_since_ms` its key had on the last board the collector built, which the collector passes to `build_board` as `BoardInput.previous_scene`; an unknown card never gains an alert and is left out of `next_escalation_at_ms`. `alert_count` counts it, so the taskbar badge keeps the alert colour and no new flash fires (the key is known); while any alerted card is unknown, the title reads `Officina (N waiting, M in alert, data unknown)` (the overlay image cannot carry text; Windows shows the title on the button) and the band ends with "Last known state: data unknown." Only a board that places the card in a known zone clears its alert. A board built without a previous one (the first of a run, `officina board --json`) has nothing to keep. Gives up: a wait that reaches its threshold while the data is stale escalates only once data is fresh. (4.3, 4.5)
- **D-495.** Reception only on `SessionStart.source = startup` (owner decision; challenge, Serious "Reception fires again"; amends AD-167 rule 4). A Your turn card is at reception when its latest `SessionStart` line carries `source` `startup` and no `UserPromptSubmit` follows it. `resume`, `clear`, `compact`, `fork` and an absent source never send a figure to reception, so a compaction or a resume leaves a veteran session in the lounge. Core folds `last_session_start_source` beside `saw_prompt`, from the event line's `source` (`events.rs`, E2's documented values). Gives up: a `/clear` reading as a fresh arrival. (4.3)
- **D-496.** Approval-only waits (owner decision; challenge, Serious "An approval-only wait escalates"; amends AD-167 rule 3, D-464, D-465). A pending relay approval makes a card waiting only while the card's column is Working, the observable fact closest to `officina run` still blocking on it (it waits 110 s by default, then the session moves on). An approval-only wait never escalates by time, is left out of `next_escalation_at_ms`, and alerts only by priority; its `waiting_since_ms` stays the request's `requested_at_ms`, for the file's "waiting since". A request left pending after its run gave up stays in the header pill and on the board's approval card, where it already is, and its session goes to the lounge or its office. Needs you plus an approval keeps Needs you's rules. (4.3, 4.8)
- **D-497.** Story order and split (owner decisions; challenge, Serious "Alert and taskbar wait" and "Story 4.6 is three stories"; replaces D-485). **Alert first**: the alert band, the window title, the taskbar badge and its flash need only core's alert state, so they ship as story 4.5, right after 4.3; the alert room's drawing and its beacon are their own small story, 4.9. **Placed first, walking second**: story 4.7 draws the scene with every figure placed at its spot at once, a complete product that the first layout of every run and reduced motion need anyway; story 4.10 adds walking: the corridor graph, the routes, the mover, the walk cycle, the depth sort's hysteresis, fades, walking off and reduced motion. Story 4.6 keeps geometry, spots, layout, memory, words and arrows; the navigation graph and `paths.ts` move to 4.10, their only user. **Order**: Slice 0: 4.1. Slice A: 4.2; then 4.3 and 4.4 side by side; 4.5; 4.6; 4.7; then 4.8 and 4.9 side by side; 4.10; 4.11. Slice B: 4.12 (sub-agents, only after gate 1 passes), 4.13 (dark theme). 4.14 closes the PRD. (all)
- **D-498.** Story numbers (D-497). A story number in parentheses in the sections below reads through D-460's map, then through this one: 4.1 to 4.4 keep theirs; the v2 numbering's 4.5 is now 4.6 (its graph and paths: 4.10), 4.6 is 4.7 (its walking: 4.10), 4.7 is 4.8, 4.8 is 4.5 (its alert room and beacon: 4.9), 4.9 is 4.12, 4.10 is 4.11, 4.11 is 4.13, 4.12 is 4.14. (all)
- **D-499.** Slice 0 gate 4, what a real wait emits (owner decision; challenge, Serious "Time escalation is not checked until the epic closes"; moves the closure story's former AC 11 into story 4.1; amends D-479). Before story 4.3 starts, the owner records, in an interactive session, which hook events arrive during a real question (`AskUserQuestion`) and a real permission prompt, and how long after the wait begins the first blocking `Notification` arrives; and counts, over the days the hook buffer on disk holds, the Needs you causes per day (`notification_type` of the `Notification` lines). If a question emits no blocking notification, its wait has no known start and never escalates by time (D-465 as written): the entry says so, and story 4.3's vectors carry the observed shape. The count tunes the alert to the waits that actually happen, in the owner's `bypassPermissions` setup. (4.1, 4.3)
- **D-500.** Gate 3 withdrawn (challenge, Worth raising "Gate 3 measures the prototype's harness"; amends D-479, D-480). The prototype's harness runs a frame loop on every frame, flickers screens per tool, ticks a clock and redraws a log panel, none of which is ported, so its figure says little about the mover. Walking ships on by default (D-469), and AD-71's walking run on the real window, in story 4.14, decides: if it fails, `DEFAULT_WALKING` becomes `false` in the same change (D-480). (4.10)

## Motion and halo

- **D-108.** `lib/halo.ts` keeps, per `(card key, enteredAt)`, `{ startMs, iterations }`: `startMs` is the first-sight time rounded down to a multiple of the 4 s cycle, and `iterations` the smallest odd count of 2 s halves with `startMs + iterations x 2000 >= enteredAt + HALO_BREATHING_MS`, or 0 when the wait is already older than `HALO_BREATHING_MS` at first sight. Each halo element, in the scene's layer 2 and on the board card, takes `animation-delay: startMs - mountMs` once, when it mounts, through a Svelte action of `lib/halo.ts`; nothing rewrites it while it stays mounted, and the action re-applies only when the pair changes (Svelte calls `update` on every new parameter object). This refines AD-65's `-(firstSeenMs mod 4000)`, which keeps the shared phase and the two-minute end only at first sight: a remount (Board to Scene and back, Focus off) restarted the breathing, out of phase. `enteredAt` is `last_event_at_ms`, else `started_at_ms`, else the first-sight time. One instance, `haloMemo`, exported by `lib/halo.ts`, serves the action and `BoardStore`, which drops the keys no longer in Needs you when a board arrives; it is a plain `Map`, not reactive state, so pruning never re-renders, and the action reads `mountMs` from the memo's injected clock (review F19). The resting opacity is one custom property, `--halo-rest`, used by the keyframes' `to` and by the halo's base style (review F23). `HALO_BREATHING_MS` is the one constant FR-9's fallback sets to 0. (4.2)
- **D-109.** Svelte 5 runs `transition` and `animate` through the Web Animations API (`element.animate`, read in svelte 5.57.3's `transitions.js`), which the global `prefers-reduced-motion` rule of `app.css` does not stop, and skips the Web Animation only when both duration and delay are 0. `lib/scene/motion.ts` therefore returns 0 for every duration and every delay while `matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)")` matches, reading `.matches` at each call so a setting changed while the window runs applies at once, as the CSS rule does (review F20). - **D-110.** In `src/lib/scene/**` and `src/components/scene/**`, `no-restricted-globals` lists `setInterval`, `setTimeout` and `requestAnimationFrame`, and `no-restricted-properties` lists `Date.now` (AD-64) and, for AD-60's purity, `Math.random`.
